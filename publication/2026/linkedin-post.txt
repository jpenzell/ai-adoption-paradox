You can use AI every day and still dislike it.

In the 2026 Stack Overflow survey, 54% of current AI users who described their attitude as very unfavorable reported daily use on at least one of four AI tool types. Among the very favorable, it was 93%.

That's the finding I keep coming back to in my analysis of the 2025 and 2026 survey data. A usage number doesn't tell you how someone feels about the work they're doing with the tool.

There is still a substantial relationship between attitude and frequency. In the models I compared, attitude distinguished daily from less frequent use better than the survey's trust question did.

And trust had conditions. Nearly half of respondents in the trust analysis said they trust AI when they can easily verify the output.

For anyone running an AI rollout, I'd put three questions next to the adoption number:

How do people feel about using it?
Which tasks are they comfortable using it for?
How do they check the result?

Then look at the quality of the work. Frequency alone can't answer that.

One important limit: Stack Overflow changed the use, attitude, and trust questions in 2026. These are different voluntary survey samples, so the year-to-year differences aren't a clean measure of change, and they don't establish cause and effect. The headline percentages are among current users with complete answers, not all developers.
